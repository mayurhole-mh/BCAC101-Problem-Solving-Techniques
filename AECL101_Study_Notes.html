Book 1: Develop Your Listening Skills

What is this book about?

This book teaches you HOW to listen properly, how English sounds work, and how to have basic conversations in English. If you want to speak good English, first you must learn to listen well!

Unit 1: Listening Skills

Simple Meaning

This unit teaches you the difference between just HEARING sounds and actually LISTENING with understanding. It also covers types of listening, barriers (things that stop you from listening well), and how to become a good listener.

Hearing vs Listening - VERY IMPORTANT!

Hearing: Your ears receive sound waves and send them to the brain. You hear but you may NOT understand. It is PHYSICAL. Example: You hear traffic noise but don't pay attention.

Listening: Your ears receive sound waves + your brain UNDERSTANDS the meaning. It is HEARING WITH A PURPOSE. Example: You hear your teacher and understand what she is saying.

Research Fact: People spend 32.7% time listening, 25.8% speaking, 22.6% writing. So listening is the MOST important skill!

6 Types of Listening

TypeSimple MeaningExample
1. DiscriminativeYou can tell the DIFFERENCE between soundsYou know the difference between Hindi and English
2. ComprehensiveYou UNDERSTAND what is being saidStudent understands the teacher's lecture
3. Evaluative (Critical)You ANALYZE if the message is good/bad, logical/illogicalDeciding if a politician's speech makes sense
4. Attentive (Active)You pay FULL attention, make eye contact, encourage the speaker - BEST type!Listening carefully to a friend's problem
5. Pretense (Passive)You LOOK like you're listening but your mind is somewhere elseNodding in class but thinking about lunch
6. SelectiveYou listen only to what YOU want to hear, ignore the restChild ignoring parents' advice but hearing "ice cream"

Note-Taking Techniques

Note-taking is NOT writing everything the speaker says. It is an ACTIVE process:

  • Listen with focus - pay attention to speaker's tone, emphasis, repetition
  • Identify key ideas - separate essential from non-essential information
  • Write in your OWN words - this helps you understand and remember better
  • Listening + Note-taking = they complement each other like two wheels of a bicycle

Barriers to Effective Listening - 2 Types

Type 1: Physical Barriers

  • Noise - loud sounds disturb concentration
  • Poor sound reproduction - bad microphone or phone connection
  • Uncomfortable seating - can't focus if you're uncomfortable
  • Message Overload (Excess Transmission) - too much information at one time, like juggling - you can only handle a few things at once
  • Passive Listening - you listen but show NO reaction, no nodding. Speaker feels discouraged.
  • Selective Listening - you hear only what you want to hear, like a teenager ignoring parents' scolding

Type 2: People-Related Barriers

  • Pre-conceived notions - already made up your mind before listening (like two politicians from different parties)
  • Preoccupation - mind is on other things (thinking about a party instead of the lecture)
  • Casual attitude - "It's not important, I'll listen with little effort"
  • Self-centered attitude - "I know everything, why should I listen to others?"
  • Disrupting the speaker - interrupting shows disinterest, creates a gap between you and speaker
  • Judge and Jump Syndrome - jumping to conclusions before the speaker finishes
  • Listening with expectations - listening only to hear what YOU want to hear (superficial listening)
  • Focusing on appearance, not speech - judging speaker's clothes/looks instead of their words
  • Wandering eyes - your eyes show you're bored or not interested

Ten Commandments of Effective Listening

  1. Stop talking - You cannot talk and listen at the same time!
  2. Put the speaker at ease - Make the speaker feel comfortable
  3. Show you want to listen - Sit still, look at the speaker, keep pen and paper ready
  4. Remove distractions - Switch off mobile, close the door, stop chewing
  5. Put yourself in the speaker's place - Be encouraging, nod sometimes
  6. Be patient - Wait for the speaker to finish, don't interrupt
  7. Do not lose your temper - Everyone has the right to speak
  8. Do not argue or criticize - If you argue, communication STOPS
  9. Ask the right questions - Meaningful questions show you are interested
  10. Do not talk, LISTEN! - Same as the first commandment - STOP TALKING!

Importance of Listening

  • Nature gave us 2 ears and 1 tongue = we should listen MORE than we speak!
  • Listening is the SINGLE most important aspect of learning
  • Attentive listening helps you understand clearly and retain information
  • Good listeners create a GOOD impression on others
  • When you don't listen = you don't learn, you miss opportunities, your mind is closed
  • "When you do not listen, there is nothing new, there are only your reactions."

Unit 2: The English Sound System

Simple Meaning

This unit teaches you how English sounds work - vowels, consonants, syllables, stress, and intonation. If you understand these, your English pronunciation will become much better!

English Sound System - Basic Facts

  • English has 44 speech sounds (phonemes) but only 26 letters
  • So there is NO one-to-one match between letters and sounds
  • Example: letter "c" = /k/ in "cat" but /s/ in "city"
  • That's why English has a separate phonetic script
  • Out of 44 sounds: 20 vowels + 24 consonants
  • Out of 20 vowels: 12 pure vowels + 8 diphthongs

Consonants (24)

Consonants are sounds made by BLOCKING or RESTRICTING the airflow. Classified by 3 features:

  • Place of articulation - WHERE the air is blocked (lips, teeth, etc.)
  • Manner of articulation - HOW the air is blocked (stopped, partially blocked)
  • Voicing - vocal cords vibrate (voiced like /b/, /d/) or not (voiceless like /p/, /t/)

The Syllable

A syllable is a sound unit BIGGER than a phoneme but SMALLER than a word. Each puff of air from your lungs produces one syllable.

4 Types of Words by Syllables:

  • Monosyllabic = 1 syllable: ear, inn, man
  • Disyllabic = 2 syllables: waiter, server
  • Trisyllabic = 3 syllables: exception, progressive
  • Polysyllabic = 4+ syllables: examination, decentralization

Syllable Structure: V = Vowel (central, must have), C = Consonant (optional)

Stress in English

English is a STRESS-TIMED language. Stress = degree of force used to pronounce a syllable. Shown by a vertical stroke (') before the stressed syllable.

3 Types of Stress:

1. Word Stress: In a multi-syllable word, one syllable is more prominent. Example: 'table (first syllable stressed). Primary stress = bar above ('); Secondary stress = bar below (,)

2. Grammatical Stress: Same word, different stress based on function!

Noun/Adjective (1st syllable)Verb (2nd syllable)
'conductcon'duct
'digestdi'gest
'objectob'ject
'presentpre'sent

3. Sentence Stress: Content words are STRESSED (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs, interrogative pronouns). Structural words are UNSTRESSED (articles, prepositions, conjunctions, auxiliary verbs, pronouns).

Example: "Rahul talked about his plans to establish a glass manufacturing unit."

Intonation Patterns

Intonation = the rise and fall of your voice pitch when speaking. We divide long sentences into tone groups (breath groups) using pauses.

ToneWhen UsedExamples
Falling ToneOrdinary statements, Wh-questions, Commands, Exclamations"She is going." / "Where are you going?" / "Shut the window." / "What a beautiful girl!"
Rising ToneYes-No questions, Incomplete statements, Polite requests, Friendly Wh-questions"Can you do it?" / "If I go there, I'll buy you a toy." / "Please be comfortable." / "How's your headache?"
Falling-Rising ToneWhen something is IMPLIED (suggested but not directly said)"She is clever (but not dependable)." / "Your essay is good (but your handwriting is not)."

  • Vowels = free airflow; Consonants = blocked/restricted airflow

Unit 5: Group Discussion

Simple Meaning

A Group Discussion (GD) is a formal discussion where a group of people talk about a specific topic. GDs are used by companies and colleges to select candidates. They test your communication skills, knowledge, and ability to work in a team.

What is Group Discussion?

  • Communication between members of a specific group on a topic of common interest
  • Usually FORMAL and TIME-BOUND
  • Used in recruitment process to check communication and interpersonal abilities
  • Employers find out if candidates can cooperate, collaborate, and work as a team

Skills Tested in GD:

  • Knowledge and communicative abilities
  • Ability to work as a team member
  • Leadership skills (leading the group towards conclusions)
  • Communication skills: starting, listening, responding, appreciating, countering, concluding

Types of GD Topics

  • Topic-based: Factual (Impact of Global Warming), Social/Political (Act of Sedition), Abstract (Faith)
  • Case-based: A situation is given, group must resolve it

How to Participate Effectively in GD

  • Keep yourself updated by reading newspapers, magazines, journals
  • Carry a notepad and pen - write down all ideas and arguments
  • Present your ideas CLEARLY and LOGICALLY
  • Make your presence felt but don't be aggressive
  • Talk to PARTICIPANTS, not to evaluators
  • Use SIMPLE, non-technical language
  • Try to present NEW ideas
  • Support arguments with EXAMPLES
  • LISTEN to others so you can respond
  • Don't interrupt others while they are speaking
  • Don't digress from the topic
  • Show agreement and disagreement when necessary
  • Conclude with a LOGICAL summary covering all points

Unit 6: Interview Skills

Simple Meaning

An interview is a formal meeting where someone asks you questions to check if you are suitable for a job or admission. This unit teaches you types of interviews, how to prepare, how to make a good impression, and what language expressions to use.

What is an Interview?

  • A formal meeting where a candidate is asked questions
  • Interviewee = the person who appears for the interview (candidate)
  • Interviewer = the person who takes the interview
  • It is a form of oral communication
  • Good qualification alone is NOT enough - your PERFORMANCE in the interview matters!

Biodata vs Resume vs CV

ResumeSHORT summary of education, work experience, skills (1-2 pages). Focused on specific job.Job applications

Types of Interviews

TypeSimple Meaning
1. Job InterviewFace-to-face or telephonic, to select a candidate for a job. Panel interviews have multiple interviewers. Multiple rounds of screening.
2. Behavioral InterviewEmployers check your PAST behaviour in situations. Questions based on actual or hypothetical situations. May check social media handles.
3. Performance Interview/ReviewPeriodic meeting between employee and supervisor to assess performance. Strategies for improvement are suggested.
4. Exit InterviewWhen an employee leaves the company. Employers get feedback. May offer incentives to retain the employee.

Interview Preparation - 9 Guidelines

  1. Research the career area you are applying for
  2. Brush up your knowledge and get updates about recent developments
  3. Read your application form carefully - anticipate questions they will ask
  4. Prepare answers for likely questions
  5. Plan your travel to the interview venue
  6. Decide your dress - dress professionally
  7. Get information about the employer - size, products, reputation, competitors, structure
  8. Prepare a file folder of all necessary documents
  9. Be positive and confident

How to Make a Good Impression

Many interviews are LOST within 3 minutes! Reasons: weak handshake, no eye contact, poor body language, wrong clothing, lack of manners.

5 Things Considered When Hiring: Work experience, First impression, Education, Professional qualification, References

  1. Ask for permission to enter the room
  2. Greet interviewers pleasantly, give a firm handshake
  3. Don't sit down until asked
  4. Make eye contact with the interviewer speaking to you
  5. Maintain proper, relaxed body posture and steady voice
  6. Listen carefully to all questions
  7. Don't interrupt the interviewer
  8. Feel confident, think positively
  9. If you don't know the answer, say so - don't give wrong answers!
  10. Use polite language
  11. Don't argue with interviewers, but make your points clearly
  12. Don't boast or show off your knowledge
  13. Wait till the interviewers tell you the interview has ended

Unit 7: Telephonic Communication

Simple Meaning

This unit teaches you how to talk on the phone properly - how to make and receive calls, put calls on hold, take and leave messages, make appointments, ask for information, and end conversations politely. Phone skills are essential because the other person CANNOT see you!

Key Difference from Face-to-Face

In telephonic communication, you CANNOT see the other person. So:

  • Body language and facial expressions have ALMOST NO effect
  • Voice aspects are VERY important: intonation, volume, clarity, speed
  • You must speak CLEARLY and CAREFULLY

Types of Calls

By Formality:

TypeExampleGreetingGoodbye
FormalBusiness call (company to dealer)"Good morning/afternoon""Goodbye."
Semi-formalColleague to colleague"Hello!""See you later."
InformalFamily/friend call"Hi!""Bye!"

By Location:

  • Local call = completed by a single exchange (same city)
  • STD call = Subscriber Trunk Dialing = connected to another exchange (different city). Dial STD code + local number.
  • International call = call to another country. Dial 9-0-1-1 + country code + city code + local number.
  • Toll-free call = numbers starting with 18 or 80, for customer service. Free of charge.

Making Calls - Key Expressions

  • "Good morning. This is Rahul. Could I speak to the Service Engineer, please?"
  • "I would like to speak to..." / "I'm trying to contact..." / "May I talk to Mr. Zubair?"
  • "I'm calling about..." / "I'm calling on behalf of Mr. Virat."

When person is unavailable:

  • "Can I leave a message for him/her?"
  • "Could you tell him/her that I called, please?"
  • "Could you ask him/her to call me back, please?"
  • "Okay, thanks. I'll call back later."

Bad connection:

  • "I think we have a bad connection. Can I call you back?"
  • "I'm sorry, could you speak a little louder, please?"

Ending the call:

  • "Thank you very much. Have a nice day." / "Thanks for your help. Have a good day."

Receiving Calls - Key Expressions

  • "(Company name). This is Sheena. How may I help you?"
  • "Good morning. (Company name). How can I help you?"
  • "Mr. Milind speaking."

Getting caller's name:

  • "May I know your name please?" / "May I ask who's calling?" / "Who am I speaking with?"

Connecting:

  • "Wait a minute please. I am trying to connect you to Mr. Sagar."
  • "Please hold and I'll put you through to Mr. Siddharth."
  • "Hang on a minute. I'll transfer your call to Mr. Rasheed."

Putting a Call on Hold

  • "Do you mind holding while I check on that?"
  • "Hold the line please." / "Could you hold on please?"
  • "Can I put you on hold for a minute?"
  • Rule: Always take the caller's PERMISSION before putting them on hold!

Taking and Leaving Messages

Taking a Message:

  • "He's not available at the moment. Would you like to leave a message?"
  • "He's out of the office right now. Can I take a message?"

Leaving a Message:

  • "Please tell Santosh that Rina called and ask him to call me back."
  • "Could you please tell Kiran to call me when she is free?"
  • "Please ask Prashant to call Deepak when he's available. It's urgent."

Rule: Always keep pen and paper near the phone to take messages!

Making Appointments

Making:

  • "I would like to make an appointment with Dr. Sagar Waghmare."
  • "Can I see the Manager?" / "I want to make an appointment to see..."

Cancelling:

  • "Sorry, but I have to cancel my appointment on Monday."
  • "Please accept my apologies. I have to cancel our appointment."
  • "Excuse me. Can I change my appointment?"

Asking for and Giving Information

Asking someone to repeat:

  • "I beg your pardon. Could you please repeat that?"
  • "I'm sorry, I can't hear you very well. Could you repeat that please?"
  • "Could you speak a little slowly, please?"
  • "Would you mind spelling that for me please?"

Bad connection:

  • "I'm sorry, we have a bad connection. Could you give me your number and I'll call you right back?"
  • "The line's very bad today. Please try later."
  • "Extremely sorry, you have the wrong number."

Ending a Conversation

  • "Is there anything else I can help you with? Okay, thanks for calling. Have a great day."
  • "Thank you for calling. Have a nice day."
  • "It's been great talking to you. I've got an appointment now, so I better go."
  • "Nice talking to you. Take care."

Rule: Don't end the call hurriedly or abruptly. End on a POSITIVE note!

Telephone Etiquettes - Important Rules!

  1. Pick up the phone PROMPTLY when it rings
  2. Introduce yourself at the beginning of the call
  3. Speak in NORMAL tone, MODERATE volume
  4. Speak CLEARLY and SLOWLY
  5. Do NOT shout - stay calm even in crisis
  6. Be a GOOD LISTENER - don't interrupt. Show attentiveness: "yes", "okay", "I see"
  7. Do NOT eat or drink while talking on the phone
  8. Ask the caller politely to repeat when you don't understand
  9. Do NOT use slang or poor language
  10. Ask BEFORE putting someone on hold. Don't keep on hold more than 30 seconds
  11. Leave SHORT and SIMPLE messages
  12. Put mobile on SILENT in meetings, cinema, restaurant, or serious functions
