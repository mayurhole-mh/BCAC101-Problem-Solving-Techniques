Book 1: The Making of the Indian Constitution

What is this book about?

This book tells the story of HOW our Constitution was made - who made it, what influenced it, what is written in the Preamble, and what makes our Constitution special.

Unit 1: The Genesis (Beginning) of the Constitution

Simple Meaning

This unit tells the story of how India's Constitution was born. It covers British rule, how Indians fought for freedom, and how the Constituent Assembly was formed to write the Constitution.

3 Stages of British Rule in India

1. Mercantile Capitalism (Trade): British came to India in 1600 for trade. Slowly they captured power. Indians didn't realize the danger because they were divided into many kingdoms.

2. Industrial Capitalism (Exploitation): British took raw materials from India (like cotton), made goods in England, and sold them back in India. This drained India's wealth. Dadabhai Naoroji called this the "Economic Drain Theory." Western education started (Lord Macaulay). Social reformers like Raja Ram Mohan Roy emerged. Indian National Congress was formed in 1885.

3. Finance Capitalism (Infrastructure): British built railways, post offices, telegraph - not to help Indians, but to strengthen their own rule. But these same things helped Indians unite and become politically aware.

Background When Constitution Was Being Made (1946-1949)

  • World War II had just ended - Hiroshima & Nagasaki bombed (1945)
  • Partition of India (1947) - lakhs died in communal riots
  • Gandhiji was assassinated in January 1948
  • India had only 15% literacy, high unemployment, food crisis
  • Despite all this chaos, the Constituent Assembly completed its work!

Formation of the Constituent Assembly

  • 1934 - M. N. Roy first proposed the idea
  • 1940 - August Offer accepted the demand in principle
  • Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) - gave framework for Assembly
  • 389 members total (296 from British India + 93 from Princely States)
  • After Partition, reduced to 299 members
  • Congress won 208 seats, Muslim League won 73 seats
  • Only 28.5% adults had voting rights at that time

Key Leaders of the Assembly

  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad - President of the Assembly
  • Dr. B. R. Ambedkar - Chairman of Drafting Committee = "Architect of the Constitution"
  • Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Patel, Maulana Azad - key Congress leaders
  • B. N. Rao - Constitutional Advisor
  • 15 women were members including Sarojini Naidu, Rajkumari Amrit Kaur, Hansa Mehta
  • Hansa Mehta changed UDHR "All men" to "All human beings are born free and equal"

Important Committees

  • Steering Committee - Dr. Rajendra Prasad (coordination)
  • Drafting Committee - Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (prepared the draft)
  • Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee - Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
  • Union-Province Relations Committees - Jawaharlal Nehru

Stages in Making the Constitution

  • 11 sessions held over nearly 3 years
  • December 1946 - Nehru introduced Objectives Resolution
  • February 1948 - Draft Constitution ready
  • Public suggestions invited through newspapers and All India Radio
  • 26 November 1949 - Constitution ADOPTED
  • 26 January 1950 - Constitution CAME INTO EFFECT (Republic Day!)

Unit 2: Sources of the Constitution of India

Simple Meaning

Our Constitution was not made from zero. It borrowed good ideas from many sources - Indian culture, other countries' constitutions, British laws, and the freedom struggle. This unit tells us WHERE each idea came from.

Sources from Indian Culture

  • Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam (Upanishads) = "The whole world is one family"
  • Buddha - "Sarve Bhavantu Sukhinah" (May all be happy); Liberty, Equality, Fraternity from Buddhist philosophy (Ambedkar said this!)
  • Sant Basaveshwara (12th century) - Anubhava Mantapa = early form of parliament
  • Mahavira - Non-violence (Ahimsa)
  • Bhakti, Sufi movements - equality, devotion beyond caste

Sources from Freedom Struggle

  • Nehru Report (1928) - first draft of Constitution by Indians (Motilal Nehru)
  • Karachi Resolution (1931) - demanded Fundamental Rights
  • Government of India Act, 1935 - biggest documentary source
  • Freedom movement gave 3 key ideas: British have no right to rule, Indians can govern themselves, every person has fundamental freedoms

Unit 3: Constitutional Philosophy (The Preamble)

Simple Meaning

The Preamble is like the "Introduction" or "Identity Card" of the Constitution. It tells us WHO made the Constitution, WHAT kind of country India is, and WHAT are the goals of the Constitution. Nani Palkhivala called it "the identity card of the Constitution."

4 Things the Preamble Tells Us

  1. WHO made it: "We, the People of India" - not the British, not the King, WE THE PEOPLE
  2. WHAT kind of country: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic
  3. WHAT are the goals: Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity
  4. WHEN adopted: 26 November 1949

5 Key Words in Preamble Explained Simply

1. SOVEREIGN = India is its own boss. No other country can tell us what to do. We make our own decisions.

2. SOCIALIST = Wealth should be shared fairly, not just with rich people. Government should help the poor. (Added by 42nd Amendment, 1976 - but the idea was always there)

3. SECULAR = Government treats ALL religions equally. No official religion. You can follow any religion or no religion. (Added by 42nd Amendment, 1976 - but the idea was always there. SC confirmed in Nov 2024 that Constitution was always secular)

4. DEMOCRATIC = People choose their government through elections. "Of the people, by the people, for the people." Ambedkar said: Democracy = bringing revolutionary changes WITHOUT bloodshed.

5. REPUBLIC = Head of state (President) is ELECTED, not born into power like a king. Every citizen can become President.

4 Goals of the Preamble

1. JUSTICE (3 types): Social (no caste discrimination) + Economic (fair wealth distribution) + Political (one person one vote)

2. LIBERTY (5 types): Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith, Worship - you are FREE to think, speak, and pray as you want

3. EQUALITY (2 types): Equality of Status (everyone equal before law, Art. 14-18) + Equality of Opportunity (equal chance for everyone, Art. 15-16)

4. FRATERNITY = Brotherhood, feeling of oneness. Ambedkar said: "Without fraternity, liberty and equality cannot survive." Fraternity ensures "dignity of the individual" and "unity and integrity of the nation."

Key Debate: "In the name of God"

H. V. Kamath wanted to add "In the name of God" to the Preamble. Vote: 68 opposed, 41 supported - REJECTED. Reason: India has many religions, this could divide people.

  • "We, the People" = people are the source of all power
  • India is: Sovereign (own boss) + Socialist (fair wealth) + Secular (all religions equal) + Democratic (people choose govt) + Republic (elected head)

  • "Socialist" and "Secular" added by 42nd Amendment (1976)

Unit 4: Salient Features of the Constitution

Simple Meaning

This unit tells us what makes our Constitution SPECIAL and DIFFERENT from other countries' constitutions. Granville Austin called it a "Social Document" because it aims to transform society.

Main Features (Remember these!)

1. Written Constitution: Largest written constitution in the world! Original: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules.

2. Sovereignty of People: "We, the People" - people are supreme, not the government.

3. Balance of Rigidity & Flexibility: Some parts easy to amend, some difficult. Article 368 = amendment procedure.

4. Parliamentary Democracy: Like Britain - PM is real head, President is nominal head. Executive responsible to Legislature.

5. Independent & Integrated Judiciary: Supreme Court → High Courts → District Courts. Independent from politics.

6. Single Citizenship: Unlike USA (state + federal), India has only ONE citizenship. You are citizen of India, not of Maharashtra or UP.

7. Fundamental Rights (Part III, Art. 12-35): 6 categories - Equality, Freedom, Against Exploitation, Religion, Cultural/Educational, Constitutional Remedies.

8. Directive Principles (Part IV, Art. 36-51): Guidelines for government - not enforceable in court but fundamental for governance. Austin called them "Conscience of the Constitution."

9. Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A, Art. 51A): 11 duties of citizens. Added by 42nd Amendment (1976). 11th duty added by 86th Amendment (2002).

10. Federal System with Strong Centre: "Union of States" (not "Federation") - Centre has more power than states. Like Canadian model.

11. Universal Adult Franchise (Art. 326): Every adult (18+) can vote. "One person, one vote, one value."

12. Secular State: No official religion. All religions treated equally.

13. Emergency Provisions: 3 types - National Emergency (Art. 352), President's Rule (Art. 356), Financial Emergency (Art. 360).

14. Three-Tier Governance: Union → State → Local Bodies (73rd & 74th Amendments added local government).

Basic Structure Doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973)

Supreme Court said: Parliament can amend the Constitution, but CANNOT change its BASIC STRUCTURE. 15 elements of basic structure include:

  • Supremacy of Constitution | Democratic & Republican form | Secular character
  • Separation of powers | Federal structure | Judicial review | Rule of law
  • Fundamental Rights | Free and fair elections | Independence of judiciary

Unit Summary - Super Simple

  • Largest written constitution in the world
  • People are supreme ("We, the People")
  • Parliamentary democracy - PM runs, President is nominal head
  • 6 Fundamental Rights + 11 Fundamental Duties + DPSP
  • Single citizenship, Universal adult franchise (18+)
  • Federal but with strong Centre ("Union of States")
  • 3 types of Emergency: National, President's Rule, Financial
  • Basic Structure CANNOT be changed (Kesavananda Bharati case)

Book 2: Indian Constitution - Our Rights and Duties

What is this book about?

This book tells us about our RIGHTS (what we can do), our DUTIES (what we must do), and how India is GOVERNED (President, Parliament, Supreme Court).

Unit 5: Fundamental Rights

Simple Meaning

Fundamental Rights are the BASIC rights that every person in India has. The government CANNOT take these away. If anyone violates your Fundamental Rights, you can go directly to the Supreme Court. Ambedkar called Article 32 "the heart and soul of the Constitution."

6 Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12-35)

RightArticlesSimple Meaning
1. Right to Equality14-18Everyone equal before law. No discrimination on religion, caste, sex, place of birth. Untouchability abolished (Art. 17). Titles abolished (Art. 18).
2. Right to Freedom19-226 freedoms: Speech, Assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Profession (Art. 19). Protection from arrest (Art. 22). Right to Life (Art. 21).
3. Right against Exploitation23-24No forced labour/human trafficking (Art. 23). No child below 14 in factory/mine (Art. 24).
4. Right to Freedom of Religion25-28Right to practice any religion (Art. 25). No tax for promoting any religion (Art. 27). No religious instruction in state schools (Art. 28).
5. Cultural & Educational Rights29-30Minorities can preserve their language/culture (Art. 29). Minorities can run educational institutions (Art. 30).
6. Right to Constitutional Remedies32If your FR is violated, go directly to Supreme Court. 5 types of Writs available.

Important Articles to Remember

  • Article 14: Equality before law
  • Article 17: Untouchability ABOLISHED
  • Article 19: 6 Freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession)
  • Article 21: Right to Life & Personal Liberty - CANNOT be suspended even during Emergency!
  • Article 21A: Free education for children 6-14 (added by 86th Amendment, 2002)
  • Article 32: Right to Constitutional Remedies - "Heart and soul" (Ambedkar)

5 Writs (Like "Weapons" to Protect Your Rights)

  1. Habeas Corpus: "Produce the body" - if someone is detained illegally, court orders them to be brought to court within 24 hours
  2. Mandamus: "We command" - court orders a government officer to do their duty
  3. Prohibition: Higher court tells lower court "STOP! You have no power to hear this case"
  4. Certiorari: Higher court CANCELS lower court's wrong decision
  5. Quo Warranto: "By what authority?" - court questions if a person has legal right to hold a public office

Important Court Cases

  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Basic Structure Doctrine - Parliament cannot change basic structure of Constitution
  • Maneka Gandhi (1978): "Procedure established by law" must be fair, just, and reasonable
  • Indra Sawhney (1992): Reservation OK but "creamy layer" must be excluded from OBC; max 50% cap
  • Vishakha (1997): SC laid down guidelines against sexual harassment at workplace
  • Puttaswamy (2017): Right to Privacy is a fundamental right under Art. 21

Key Facts for Exam

  • Originally 7 Fundamental Rights. 44th Amendment (1978) removed Right to Property. Now only 6 rights.
  • Property is now a legal right under Article 300A
  • Articles 20 & 21 CANNOT be suspended even during Emergency
  • Some rights are for ALL persons (Art. 14, 20, 21, 23, 25), some for CITIZENS only (Art. 19, 15, 16)
  • Article 13: Any law that violates FR = null and void

Unit 6: Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)

Simple Meaning

DPSP are like "instructions to the government" on how to make India a better country. They are NOT enforceable in court (you can't sue the government for not following them), but the government MUST try to follow them while making laws. Granville Austin called them "Conscience of the Constitution."

3 Types of DPSP

1. Socialist Principles (Arts. 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A, 47): Government should ensure fair distribution of wealth, equal pay for equal work, right to livelihood, maternity relief, living wage for workers.

2. Gandhian Principles (Arts. 40, 43, 43B, 46, 47, 48): Based on Gandhi's ideas - village panchayats, cottage industries, welfare of SC/ST, prohibition of alcohol, cow protection.

3. Liberal-Intellectual Principles (Arts. 44, 45, 48, 48A, 49, 50, 51): Uniform Civil Code, early childhood care, scientific agriculture, environment protection, monument protection, separation of judiciary, international peace.

Important Articles in DPSP

  • Article 38: State shall promote welfare of people
  • Article 39: Equal right to livelihood, equal pay for equal work, prevent concentration of wealth
  • Article 39A: Free legal aid (added by 42nd Amendment)
  • Article 40: Organize village panchayats
  • Article 41: Right to work, education, public assistance
  • Article 42: Maternity relief
  • Article 44: Uniform Civil Code (same laws for marriage/divorce for all religions)
  • Article 46: Promote educational/economic interests of SC/ST
  • Article 47: Improve nutrition & public health; prohibit harmful drinks/drugs
  • Article 48A: Protect environment & wildlife (added by 42nd Amendment)
  • Article 50: Separate judiciary from executive
  • Article 51: Promote international peace

Unit 7: Fundamental Duties

Simple Meaning

Just like we have Fundamental Rights (what we CAN do), we also have Fundamental Duties (what we SHOULD do). These were NOT in the original Constitution - they were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) on the recommendation of Swaran Singh Committee.

The 11 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)

Remember: 10 duties added in 1976, 11th added by 86th Amendment (2002)

  1. Respect the Constitution, National Flag, National Anthem
  2. Follow the noble ideals of our freedom struggle
  3. Protect India's sovereignty, unity, and integrity
  4. Defend the country and do national service when called
  5. Promote harmony - brotherhood beyond religion/language/region; respect women's dignity
  6. Preserve our rich cultural heritage
  7. Protect the environment - forests, rivers, wildlife; have compassion for living creatures
  8. Develop scientific temper, humanism, spirit of inquiry
  9. Safeguard public property; reject violence
  10. Strive for excellence in all spheres
  11. Provide education to your child (age 6-14) - Added by 86th Amendment (2002)

Key Points

  • Part IV-A, Article 51A
  • 3 January = Fundamental Duties Day (when they came into effect)
  • They apply to CITIZENS only (not foreigners)
  • They are NOT enforceable in court (no punishment for not following)
  • But Parliament CAN make laws to enforce them
  • Rights and Duties are "two sides of the same coin"

Unit 8: The Governance System of India

Simple Meaning

This unit tells us HOW India is run - who is the President, how Parliament works (Rajya Sabha & Lok Sabha), how the Supreme Court protects our rights, and how the Constitution can be changed (amended).

The President of India

  • Article 52: There shall be a President of India - First citizen, Supreme Commander of Armed Forces
  • Qualifications: Citizen of India, 35+ years age, qualified for Lok Sabha membership
  • Election: Indirect - by Electoral College (elected MPs + elected MLAs). Single transferable vote.
  • Tenure: 5 years. Can be re-elected.
  • Salary: Rs. 5,00,000/month
  • Oath: By Chief Justice of Supreme Court
  • Impeachment (Art. 61): Only for "violation of Constitution". Needs 2/3 majority in both Houses. Never happened in India!
  • Real position: President = nominal head (like King of England). PM = real head. President acts on advice of Council of Ministers (Art. 74).

President's Powers

  • Administrative: Appoints PM, Ministers, CAG, Attorney General, Governors, CEC, Ambassadors
  • Legislative: Part of Parliament, can return Bill once, Ordinance power (Art. 123), nominates 12 to Rajya Sabha, can dissolve Lok Sabha
  • Financial: No Money Bill without President's approval, Budget presented on behalf of President
  • Judicial: Pardoning power, appoints SC/HC judges
  • Emergency: National Emergency (Art. 352), President's Rule (Art. 356), Financial Emergency (Art. 360)

Rajya Sabha (Council of States)

  • Max 250 members: 12 nominated by President + 238 elected by State MLAs
  • 12 nominated from: literature, science, art, sports, social service
  • Age: Must be 30+ years
  • Tenure: Permanent house (cannot be dissolved). Members serve 6 years; 1/3 retire every 2 years
  • Chairman: Vice-President of India (ex-officio)
  • Special Power: If Rajya Sabha passes resolution, Parliament can make laws on State List (valid for 1 year)
  • Weakness: Cannot introduce Money Bill. Only can delay it 14 days.

Lok Sabha (House of the People)

  • Max 550 members: 530 from States + 20 from UTs (directly elected)
  • Age: Must be 25+ years
  • Tenure: 5 years (President can dissolve earlier)
  • Speaker: Presides over Lok Sabha. Decides if Bill is Money Bill.
  • STRONGEST house: Council of Ministers responsible to Lok Sabha. Money Bills only here. Can remove government by No-Confidence Motion.

Supreme Court

  • Articles 124-146 (Part V, Chapter IV)
  • 34 Judges total (1 Chief Justice + 33 others) - as per 2019 Amendment
  • Qualifications: Citizen, HC Judge for 5+ years OR HC Advocate for 10+ years
  • Appointment: By President on advice of CJI
  • Tenure: Until 65 years of age
  • Impeachment: For misbehaviour or incapacity. Needs majority + 2/3 in both Houses.
  • 5 Jurisdictions:
    1. Original (Art. 131): Disputes between Union & States
    2. Appellate: Hears appeals from High Courts
    3. Advisory (Art. 143): President can ask SC's opinion
    4. Writ (Art. 32): Issues 5 writs for FR protection
    5. Judicial Review: Can declare laws unconstitutional

Constitutional Amendment (Article 368)

  • 3 Methods:
    1. Simple Majority: For minor changes (creating Legislative Councils, MP salaries)
    2. Special Majority: 2/3 members present + voting + majority of total membership (for most provisions including FR, DPSP)
    3. Special Majority + Half State Ratification: For federal provisions (election of President, Union-State powers, SC jurisdiction)
  • No Joint Sitting for Amendment Bills - must pass separately in both Houses
  • Basic Structure CANNOT be changed (Kesavananda Bharati case)
  • Over 100 amendments so far
